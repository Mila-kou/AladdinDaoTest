## 建议 1（必改）：3.7 节剩余价值公式 PnL 符号错误
**原文（3.7 节）**
> Residual Value = Margin − PnL − Funding − Liquidation Fee − Close Fee − Execution Impact
**问题**
PnL 前为减号。在产品文档其他位置，PnL 指「带符号的盈亏」（盈利为正、亏损为负）。被清算的仓位 PnL 通常为负，`− PnL` 会把亏损反向加回，方向错误，且与全文的 PnL 口径不一致。
**建议改法**
```javascript
剩余价值 = 保证金 + PnL − 资金费 − 清算费 − 平仓费 − 执行冲击
（PnL 为带符号盈亏：盈利为正、亏损为负）
```

## 建议 2（必改）：4.2 节清算费 0.5% 过高，与清算线冲突
**原文（4.2 节费率表）**
> Liquidation fee — 0.5% of notional size
**问题**
清算费 0.5% 与清算线几乎相等，会导致用户被清算时保证金被清算费吃光、退回值趋近 0：
1. **清算线不含清算费**：`isPositionLiquidatable()`（`PositionUtils.sol:364-365`）显式传 `isLiquidation = false`，注释为 *"should not account for liquidation fees to determine if position should be liquidated"*。清算触发条件为 `剩余保证金 < notional × minCollateralFactor`，即清算线约为 notional 的 **0.4%（BTC）/ 0.5%（ETH）**。
2. **清算费在执行时照扣**：清算成交时（`DecreasePositionCollateralUtils.sol:112` 传 `isLiquidation = true`）按 `notional × liquidationFeeFactor` 收取清算费（`PositionPricingUtils.sol:537 getLiquidationFees`）。
3. 因此当 **清算费(0.5%) ≈ 清算线(0.4\~0.5%)** 时，触发清算瞬间剩余保证金几乎全部转为清算费，用户拿回约 0，甚至不足以覆盖清算费。

## 建议 3（建议改）：3.5 节资金费「按小时收取」表述不准确
**原文（3.5 节）**
> FX100 charges hourly funding based on an EMA of system skew.
**问题**
两处歧义：
1. 偏斜 EMA 的平滑窗口为 **30 分钟**（`FundingSkewEmaMinutes: 30`，可配），并非 "hourly"。
2. 资金费并非「每小时收一次」，而是**持续累计、在每次仓位交互（开仓/平仓/调整）时按实际经过时间结算**，结算时点不固定。"hourly" 易被理解为固定整点扣费。
**建议改法**
> 资金费率根据系统偏斜的 EMA 平滑计算并**持续累计**，在每次仓位交互时按实际经过时间结算（而非固定按小时收取）。EMA 仅用于平滑偏斜，使费率变化更稳定；费率本身随偏斜动态变化。

**原文（3.5 节）**
> A floor fee covers the minimum protection cost on long positions ...
**问题**
该句把「保护成本只加在多头」作为既定事实，但未说明为何只对多头收取，且与第 2 节「每个仓位开仓即享 15 分钟免费保护」在直觉上冲突，读者易误以为保护并不免费。

## FX100 — Non-Liquidatable Perpetual Trading System
**Document:** Product overview
**Network:** Base — USDC settlement, Chainlink Data Streams
**Status:** Private Testnet
### 1. Overview
FX100 is a perpetual futures system built by f(x) Protocol, a product line of AladdinDAO. Its defining characteristic is that it does not rely on forced liquidation as the default response to adverse price movement. Protection is embedded directly into each position, so a position can survive short-term volatility that would otherwise trigger a forced close.
All economic activity — trade execution, funding, fees, and protection — is routed through a single on-chain liquidity vault. Pricing is anchored to Chainlink Data Streams and validated against Pyth price feeds, margin is isolated per position, and all markets are collateralised and settled in USDC. FX100 supports leverage of up to 100x and launches on Base.
#### 1.1 Design rationale
On a conventional perpetual exchange, a position is closed once its equity falls below the maintenance margin. Because brief volatility spikes, oracle wicks, and thin-liquidity moments can breach that threshold momentarily, positions are routinely closed that would have remained solvent shortly afterward. FX100 removes forced liquidation as the default outcome: every position opens with the first 15 minutes of liquidation protection at no cost, during which the protection engine absorbs short-term volatility rather than closing the position. The system's primary objective is to maintain the solvency of user positions through high volatility.
The unified liquidity vault is the sole counterparty to every trade. There are no external market makers and no external liquidator bonus, so no party profits from forcing a position to close — liquidations are never hunted. Liquidation protection covers the first 15 minutes of a position; after that window a position can still be liquidated, but only against the vault at the oracle price, never by a third party with an incentive to trigger it. Together with leverage of up to 100x, this makes FX100 a safe haven for high-leverage traders: a free protection window at entry, and a structure in which liquidations — when they do occur — cannot be hunted or manipulated.
### 2. Protection Engine
The protection engine maintains position solvency under adverse conditions by managing exposure continuously, rather than waiting for a margin threshold to be breached.
#### 2.1 Position structure
Each position is defined by five components:
- **Margin:** the collateral backing the position.
- **Notional exposure:** the leveraged size.
- **Liquidation threshold:** the equity level at which a standard liquidation applies when protection is off.
- **Funding term:** the position's ongoing funding obligation.
- **Liquidation protection term:** the initial 15-minute protection covering the position.
#### 2.2 Continuous risk containment
Protection logic runs continuously and adjusts exposure dynamically as the market moves, keeping risk inside protocol-defined safety margins and preventing liquidation events under normal volatility. Rather than reacting to a single breach point, the system manages the position's risk profile in real time.
#### 2.3 Position continuity
Every position includes the first 15 minutes of liquidation protection at no cost. During this window the protection engine actively manages the position's risk so that ordinary short-term volatility does not force a close. After the 15-minute window the position no longer carries free protection and becomes subject to the standard liquidation process described in Section 3.7.
#### 2.4 Extended protection (planned)
A future upgrade will let traders extend liquidation protection beyond the initial 15-minute window by paying a fee — functionally similar to purchasing liquidation insurance on a position. This gives traders the option to hold protected positions over longer horizons rather than only at entry. Extended protection is not available on the current Private Testnet and is planned for a later iteration.
### 3. System Architecture
#### 3.1 Universal liquidity vault
Liquidity providers deposit USDC into a single unified vault that backs every perpetual market on FX100. The vault is the sole counterparty to trades, which removes the need to bootstrap separate liquidity per market and lets capital be used efficiently across the whole system. The vault accepts USDC exclusively.
- Market-specific exposure limits cap how much risk any single market can take on.
- Diversification across markets reduces concentration risk for LPs.
- A four-day withdrawal period applies, during which capital continues to participate in market-making.
LPs are compensated from open and close fees, funding fees, liquidation fees, and residual system revenue, so their yield scales with the activity and risk they absorb.
#### 3.2 Isolated margin and collateral
FX100 uses an isolated-margin model. Each position is backed only by its own margin, losses are contained within that position, and risk and protection logic are applied independently per position. This simplifies risk attribution, prevents cross-contamination between positions, and aligns with established isolated-margin perpetual practice. USDC is supported as collateral at launch.
#### 3.3 Oracle and pricing framework
FX100 uses Chainlink Data Streams as its primary oracle and validates it against Pyth price feeds. If the two sources deviate beyond a defined threshold, opening, closing, and liquidation actions for the affected asset are paused automatically; existing positions remain protected during the pause, and normal operation resumes once pricing realigns. This dual-source validation defends users against stale, manipulated, or disrupted feeds.
#### 3.4 Dynamic spread mechanism
Instead of replicating a centralized order book, FX100 infers liquidity conditions and converts them into a smooth, deterministic execution-price adjustment. The guiding principle is that large trades should pay for the risk they introduce, while trades that reduce systemic imbalance should pay less. The spread has three additive components:
> **Dynamic Spread = Base Spread + Liquidity Impact + Position Imbalance Impact**
- **Base spread:** a per-asset constant representing the minimum cost of accessing leverage, independent of trade size.
- **Liquidity impact:** scales trade size against the estimated notional depth required to move the asset by 1%, producing a convex curve: negligible cost for small trades, proportionally greater cost for large ones.
- **Position imbalance impact:** a skew adjustment based on global open-interest imbalance. Trades that increase imbalance pay more; trades that reduce it are rewarded. The sensitivity coefficient can rise during extreme conditions to keep LP exposure bounded and correct imbalance quickly.
#### 3.5 Funding mechanism
FX100 charges hourly funding based on an EMA of system skew. Whichever side contributes more systemic risk pays funding to the other: when the system is net long, longs pay and shorts receive; when net short, the reverse. A floor fee covers the minimum protection cost on long positions, but skew can offset or reverse the net result. LPs always receive a share of total funding as compensation for absorbing platform-level directional and volatility risk. Together, the dynamic skew spread, hourly funding, and the LP funding share form a single risk-neutralization engine that keeps the platform balanced and solvent across market regimes.
#### 3.6 Auto-deleveraging (ADL)
ADL is a last-resort mechanism, intentionally rare. It activates only when pending profits across a market approach a safety threshold relative to the collateral backing it — typically during extreme volatility or heavily one-sided open interest. When triggered, the protocol ranks positions by unrealized profit and partially or fully closes the highest-profit positions at the prevailing oracle price until pending profit falls back below the threshold. Traders receive the realized PnL on any portion closed. ADL never produces negative balances and never affects users with no open position; it is a protective measure for system solvency, not a penalty.
#### 3.7 Liquidation after the protection window
Once a position's 15-minute protection window ends, it becomes subject to a standard margin-based liquidation process. A position becomes liquidatable when its equity falls below the minimum margin requirement, at which point it is force-closed against the liquidity vault at the oracle price — insulating the unwind from short-term volatility and thin liquidity. A liquidation fee and a close fee (each a percentage of notional) are deducted, along with any execution impact from unwinding into the vault. Residual collateral, if any, is returned to the trader:
> **Residual Value = Margin − PnL − Funding − Liquidation Fee − Close Fee − Execution Impact**
There is no external liquidator bonus — all liquidation accounting is internal and oracle-based.
### 4. Trading
#### 4.1 Limit orders
Traders can set an exact entry or exit price. The system monitors the oracle price continuously, and once it reaches the specified level the order becomes eligible for execution against the liquidity vault at the prevailing oracle price, subject to slippage and execution-impact settings. Execution is not guaranteed (it depends on keeper execution, liquidity, oracle updates, and the trader's slippage tolerance) and open orders can be viewed, edited, or cancelled at any time before they trigger.

### 5. Supported Markets and Collateral
FX100 launches with perpetual markets on a focused set of liquid assets:
**BTC, ETH, SOL, HYPE, LINK, SUI, NEAR, AERO, WLD, VVV, ZEC, and XMR.**
All markets are collateralised and settled in USDC, and the liquidity vault that backs them accepts USDC exclusively. Additional markets will be introduced over time.
### 6. Economic and Solvency Design
FX100's economics are structured to keep the system solvent and liquidity incentivised across market cycles. System fees are distributed so that the majority flows to liquidity providers, a portion funds an insurance reserve that strengthens protection against unforeseen events, and a further reserve is used to top up LP returns during lower-volume periods, smoothing earnings across cycles. All allocations are adjustable by governance as the protocol evolves.
This design ties LP yield directly to the activity and risk they underwrite, while the insurance reserve and the ADL backstop protect the system's solvency at the tail.
### 7. About f(x) Protocol
FX100 is built by f(x) Protocol, a decentralized finance protocol developed within AladdinDAO. f(x)'s existing product suite centres on fxUSD, a decentralized stablecoin, alongside yield, minting, and leveraged-position products. FX100 extends that suite into perpetual trading, applying the same emphasis on risk containment and on-chain solvency.
